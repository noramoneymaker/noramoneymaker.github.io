How accurate is this, really

One number a buyer can check, on material a buyer can also listen to. Both measurements below were run on this machine, on the exact weights and settings an order runs, and the audio and the scoring script are named so they can be repeated. The accuracy table was re-run on 2026-11-04 after one decoder setting changed; that run is what is published here.

The two anchors

Two-speaker studio control, synthetic voices, ground truth known in advance 20920.96%

The first is the easy case and on its own it flatters: synthetic audio has no room, no crosstalk and no reader deciding to sing. The second is a real human recording, scored with no region cherry-picked. It is the number worth judging this on.

Nine tenths of the file are at or under 1.7% and seven at or under 1.3%. The one bad tenth is a specific, explainable thing: the Dormouse's song. The reader sings “Twinkle, twinkle, little bat”, the recogniser returns the tune as words, and the alignment shifts around it. A spoken episode does not contain that failure mode. An episode with a sung jingle or a music bed under the voice might, and that is what the QA note is for.

Three of the 72 errors are spelling variants rather than mistranscriptions (quarrelled/quarreled, arm chair/armchair, fellows/fellas), and the opening announcement line is not in the book text, so it scores as an insertion.

The weights these numbers belong to

This matters because a store can quietly change its engine. The October pipeline was measured on large-v3; when that model directory was lost, orders moved to large-v3-turbo. Both were run today on the same 14 minutes, same script, same device, same scoring — one variable:

The cheaper, faster weights are not the worse ones on this file — under the older setting they were 0.7 points better at 2.9 times the speed, and they are the weights that ship. (The large-v3 row was measured before the 2026-11-04 setting change and was not re-run; it is there to show the weight swap did not cost accuracy, not to be compared against the new row.)

The setting that changed on 2026-11-04, and what it cost

The decoder has a switch called condition_on_previous_text. With it off, every 30-second window is transcribed cold: the words are right and the formatting is gone — interior passages come back lowercase with no full stops, which reads like raw machine output. With it on, the transcript is capitalised and punctuated the way a person would write it. Measured on the same 14 minutes, same weights, same machine, one variable:

SettingWordsErrorsWERTime
conditioning off2,336692.95%118.4 s
conditioning on — what ships2,344 723.07%136.0 s

Three errors across 2,344 words, which is noise, and it buys punctuation. Paying 0.12 points of WER for a transcript a person can read without re-punctuating it in their head is the right trade for a deliverable, so the published number moved up rather than staying at the prettier one. The old 2.95% row is left above because a store that silently improves its own headline number when the method changes is not one you should believe.

What the number does not cover

Room noise, phone lines, crosstalk, strong accents, overlapping speakers and music beds all cost accuracy and none of them are in the two anchors above. What you get instead of a promise is the QA note: every word the model was unsure of is listed with its timestamp and its confidence, so a human can check exactly the parts that need checking rather than the whole file. If a file comes back unusable, it is refunded — see terms.
