What you receive

FileWhat it is
transcript.txt Plain text, one line per speaker turn, speaker labels attributed per word and a timestamp on every turn.
transcript-reading.txt The same text with the timestamps stripped, for reading or pasting into a show-notes draft.
episode.srt / .vtt Subtitles built to a delivery spec: max 2 lines, 42 characters per line, 7 s max cue, 0.84 s min cue, 2-frame gap. The violation count is measured per file, not asserted.
cues.json / words.json Every cue and every word with its own start, end and confidence — so you can re-cut without re-transcribing.
QA.txt Every low-confidence word named, with its timestamp. Nothing is silently guessed.

How it works

  1. Email noramoneymaker@proton.me with the file, or a link to it.
  2. You get back the price and the wallet address for your order (that is the whole checkout).
  3. Send USDC on Base or Solana, reply with the transaction hash.
  4. The files arrive by email, usually the same day and always inside 24 hours.

Your audio is processed on one local machine. It is not uploaded to a transcription service, a cloud API or a model vendor. The uploaded file and the working copy are deleted when the deliverables are sent — only the deliverables survive.

Why this instead of a $0.20/minute machine service

Those exist, and they are cheaper. What they do not give you is a per-word speaker attribution, a QA note that names the words it is unsure about, or a promise that the audio was never handed to a third party. If your guests did not agree to a vendor holding their voices, that last point is the one that matters.

What this honestly is not

It is not a hosted product with a status page. It is one operator with one GPU and a pipeline, and the delivery is an email. Measured accuracy on clean studio narration is under 1% word error rate; a phone interview with crosstalk and room noise will be worse than that, and the QA note is there to tell you where. If a file comes back unusable, say so and it is refunded — terms.
